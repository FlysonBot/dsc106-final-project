About the data

This prototype uses the team's shared state_monthly_tas.csv for all 50 states. As documented in our proposal, the source is CMIP6 CanESM5, historical experiment, r1i1p1f1, Amon/tas, native grid, version v20190429 (1850–2014). Monthly temperatures were averaged within states using grid-cell overlap with 2024 U.S. Census state boundaries. The model's coarse resolution limits local detail, especially for small and island states.

For each state and year, we take the maximum and minimum of twelve monthly mean temperatures and subtract them. The hottest and coldest months can differ across states and years. A smaller gap alone does not tell us whether the hottest month cooled or the coldest month warmed.

Prototype progress

What have you done so far?

We connected our existing monthly temperature dataset to the webpage and checked that every state-year has twelve months of data. We calculated each annual gap by subtracting the coldest monthly mean temperature from the hottest monthly mean temperature. We built a D3 map with a fixed color scale and a year slider that updates the state colors. We added play and pause controls to move through the years. Selecting a state updates a line chart and displays its hottest month, coldest month, and gap for the selected year.

What will be the most challenging part of your project to design, and why?

The most challenging part will be helping readers distinguish long-term changes from year-to-year variation. A changing map can show where gaps are large, but it does not directly show whether those gaps are increasing or decreasing. We plan to compare the first and last thirty years and show changes in the hottest and coldest months separately. This comparison will need clear labels because the size of a gap and the change in a gap are different quantities. We also need to communicate the limits of a single climate model without making the explanation difficult to follow.
